Tremor

震 shin

To shake, to tremble. It is written with rain above, and began as the word for a thunderclap, the kind that shakes all things.

In Japanese an earthquake is 地震, jishin: the shaking of the ground.

Listening to the ground.

Listening to the seismographs.

Tremor replays every earthquake the world's seismographs have reported over the past day, or the past week, in a minute or two.

Each one sends out an echo from where it struck. The stronger it was, the wider and redder the echo, and the more rings it leaves: one for a tremor, two from magnitude 3, three from magnitude 5.
